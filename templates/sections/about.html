I studied Computer Science and Mathematics at the University of Prince Edward Island, then completed my Master of Data Science and Artificial Intelligence at the University of Waterloo. Somewhere along the way I realized what I enjoy most: taking messy, complicated data and turning it into something people can actually see and use.

My path has wandered through a few worlds. It started with a role as a cybersecurity analyst at Invesco, where I learned the fundamentals of security and got hands-on experience with SIEM tools. Later I worked as a developer and IT analyst, where I got to build a variety of projects internal tools, a CRM, monitoring systems and many other things, while collaborating with my teams. During my Master's at UWaterloo, I worked on research projects with Professor Carl Haas and Professor Parsin Haji Reza. With Professor Haas, I trained AI models to segment industrial point clouds and benchmarked them against other methods . Ths work has been submitted to the Journal of Construction Engineering and Management and is under review for publication. With Professor Haji Reza, I worked with generative adversarial networks (GANs) for virtual staining in cancer imaging.

These days I build digital twins at PACE Building Technologies. You can think of a digital twin as a replica of something in the real world. Imagine exploring a building or an industrial site as a walkable virtual scene. In my current role I work in Unity, where I've built a pipeline that brings massive point clouds, Gaussian splats, and CAD models together in real time, letting users explore the scene, take measurements, and move objects around, with collision detection as they go. If you're looking for someone for computer vision work, Unity or 3D development, AI and machine learning projects, or data-heavy software in general, that's exactly the kind of work I take on.
